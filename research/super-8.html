Super 8, read line by line

What the strategy does, bar by bar, read from its Pine source. And the question that comes first: is it a martingale?

Script: Super 8 - 30M BTC by AssassinsGrid · Mozilla Public License 2.0 · last update 9 Feb 2024 · read on 13 Nov 2026

The short answer

It is not a martingale. It averages into a position against the move, with a cap, a hard stop and a loss known before the first entry. A martingale doubles after a loss and has no floor. Here the size grows in a straight line, stops at three entries, and the full position loses about 5% of the balance at the stop.

The setting

Market
BTCUSDT perpetual on OKX, 30-minute bars, long and short.
Costs
0.05% commission per side. No slippage.
Margin
1.5%, so up to about 66× leverage is allowed.
Fills
Orders fill at the close of the bar that triggers them.
Inputs
About thirty, all tuned by the author on the whole BTC history.

How it enters

Two entries. Either one is enough. The short side is the mirror image.

The trend entry All five at once:
  • EMA 100 over EMA 500, and the EMA 500 rising.
  • DMI on the long side (+DI over −DI), with ADX over 20.5 (length 28, smoothing 9).
  • Parabolic SAR under the close (0.1, 0.04, 0.4).
  • MACD histogram above zero (24, 52, 11).
  • Volume over 0.4 times its 55-bar average.
The dip entry All four at once:
  • The low crosses under the lower Bollinger band (40, 2.2).
  • The same EMA trend as above.
  • DMI not on the short side with ADX over 20.5.
  • Bands at least 5% wide.

How it adds

  • At most three entries in one position.
  • Every add needs a fresh signal from either entry. For an add, the bands only need to be 2% wide.
  • Every add needs a price at least 0.7% better than the last entry.
  • Sizes grow 1 : 2 : 3. The first entry is one sixth of the full position, the second two sixths, the third three sixths.

How it exits

Stop
The closer of two levels. 8% from the average entry. An ATR stop, 15 times the 14-bar ATR, that follows the price and never moves back.
Target
A trailing exit that switches on past the farther of two levels: the 55-bar Donchian high, or the average entry plus 1.8%. From there it trails 0.3% behind the price.
After an add
Stop and target are computed again from the new average entry.
Opposite signal
An order the other way, netted against the open position: it can shrink it, close it or flip it.

How it sizes

  • Each entry is sized so the full position loses 5% of the balance if the stop is hit.
  • The size comes from that 5% divided by the distance to the stop, so a closer stop means a bigger position.
  • The balance includes realized profit, so the size compounds.

Weak points

Read the backtest with care About thirty inputs tuned on the whole history of one asset. Nothing was held out to test them on. A 0.3% trail on 30-minute bars, with the bar magnifier off. TradingView cannot see the path inside the bar, so the backtest is too kind. The author says so in a tooltip. No slippage on any fill, the stops included.
  • On a bar that gives a dip add while a long is open, the script cancels its exit orders. Until the next close the position has no stop.
  • An opposite signal is sized with the formula for a new position, counting the trades still open on the other side. What it leaves open is hard to predict.
  • pyramiding = 0 in the header does nothing. The script uses strategy.order, which ignores it, and counts the entries itself.

Porting it to Phisys

The plan for a faithful copy.

  1. One signal module with the two entries, so an add can come from either one.
  2. Three management options: sizes that grow from one tranche to the next, the add distance in % from the last entry, and a trailing exit with an activation price.
  3. The strategy file, built from the module and those options, with the stop from the average entry.

Two things stay as Phisys does them. Risk is measured on the initial capital, never compounded. Exit levels inside a bar are resolved on 5-minute bars, which removes the trailing stop's free ride.
