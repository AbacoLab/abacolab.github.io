Liquidation Heatmap: how we chose the model

Where would open positions be liquidated? No exchange publishes it, so every heatmap is an estimate. We rebuilt the best-known ones, tested them against the liquidations that actually happened, and kept what read them best.

In short

We rebuilt Coinglass's map from our own data, close to cell by cell.Tested against real liquidations, its rule barely beats the same map with its timing scrambled: it does not know where positions are.Our map read the real liquidations better than Coinglass's rule in 287 of 322 cases, on every crypto of three exchanges.Against Alphractal's map, on 89 coins: ours read them better on 69 over the next day, and on 62 over the next three days.Against Kingfisher, on the BTC maps we could compare: a tie.We tried letting positions close before the price reaches them. Every way made the map worse, over two and a half months and over almost three years.A heatmap shows where a move would find liquidations. It does not say the price will go there.

Positions that close

A position can be closed by its owner long before the price reaches its liquidation level.We estimated those closures six ways: by age, with lives from six hours to a week; from the flow of trading; and from the open interest that leaves the market.None helped. The shorter the life we gave positions, the worse the map read.

Limits

Every map here is an estimate: no exchange publishes where its positions are.Two and a half months is all the history we hold with the exact price of each liquidation; the three-year reading uses a coarser record, checked against the exact one first.Windows overlap, so the counts are not independent. These are indications, not proof.
