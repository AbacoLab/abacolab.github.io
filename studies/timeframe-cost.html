What win rate does your timeframe ask for?

Before you choose a timeframe, find out what it costs you.
We measured it on 89 Hyperliquid crypto perps, so you can look up your own.

Already trading? Skip the basics and go to the results ↓

The basics

Four short sections with a drawing each: what a trade costs, and why volatility decides how much.

Start with a trade

Say you open a trade and decide in advance how it ends. If it goes against you, you lose $100. If it goes your way, you make $200.

Traders call the $100 you risk 1 R, for risk, and a trade like this a 2:1 trade. We will use R from here on.

With no costs at all, you break even by winning one trade out of three: one win brings $200, two losses take $200.

But a trade is never free. Fees and spread are paid every time, win or lose. So you have to win more than one out of three, and how much more is exactly what this study measures.

In this example we put our stop loss at one ATR, how far on average a bar usually moves. It is how we measure volatility: how much an asset moves.

What a trade really costs

Every time you enter or leave with a market order, Hyperliquid takes a fee of 0.045% of the size of your order. A market order takes the price that is already there, and that makes you a taker.

Traders count these small percentages in basis points, bps: 1 bps is 0.01%. So the fee is 4.5 bps, and getting in and out costs 9.

Then there is the spread: you buy a little above the price you can sell at.

Fees plus spread are the cost of the round trip. BTC pays 9 bps, the typical asset 18, and the thinnest coins up to 88.

Volatility also decides how much you spend

Fees and spread are charged on the size of your position, not on what you risk.

And the size of your position depends on volatility. To risk $100 with the stop one ATR away, a calm asset forces you into a bigger position: the stop is so close that you need a large size for a small move to hurt.

Take 5-minute bars. To risk $100 on BTC you open about $78,000. On HYPE, which moves much more, about $29,000.

The costs are charged on those sizes: $72 on BTC and $30 on HYPE. That is 0.72 R against 0.30 R.

So HYPE is the cheaper trade, even if it costs more per dollar traded (11 bps against 9): it moves enough to need a much smaller position.

TRX is the extreme. It barely moves on a 5-minute bar: to risk $100 you open about $156,000, and the costs, $190, are higher than what you risk.

What counts is not what you pay per dollar. It is how far the asset moves for the dollars you put in.

Volatility can be dangerous, but it makes your trading cheaper. Very volatile moments also bring a wider spread. To choose an asset, balance the two: enough volatility, a tight spread, and the lowest fee you can get.

Why the timeframe changes it

The same logic works across timeframes. Longer bars are more volatile bars: the ATR grows, so the stop goes further away.

On BTC one ATR is about 13 bps on a 5-minute bar and 114 on a 4-hour bar. A farther stop means a smaller position for the same $100.

On 5-minute bars you need about $78,000 and pay $72. On 4-hour bars, about $8,800 and you pay $8. Same asset, same fees, a tenth of the cost.

The results

What 89 Hyperliquid crypto perps say

So, how often do you have to win just to not lose money to costs? Without costs, 33% would be enough on any timeframe. With them, on the typical crypto perp, you need 56% on 5-minute bars, 45% on 15-minute bars, 38% on 1-hour bars and 36% on 4-hour bars. The shorter the bars, the more of your winnings the costs eat.

Before any edge.

Cheap is not cheap in risk

The cost per dollar traded and the win rate you need do not go together: BTC is the cheapest of the 89, and still needs 57% on 5-minute bars.

How long the price takes

Volatility, in minutes: how long does the price take to go 1% away from where it is? On the typical asset, 1h20. BTC takes 6h30, HYPE 1h10, TRX 15h.

A fast asset lets you use short timeframes. A slow one does not, however cheap it looks.

What to do with it

Look up your asset and your timeframe before you trade. If the win rate it needs is more than you can believe, change one of the two.
  • Put the stop further away. The position shrinks, and so do the costs. At two ATRs they halve, and so does the extra win rate.
  • Use limit orders when you can. A limit order waits to be filled instead of crossing the spread: you are a maker. Hyperliquid charges makers 1.5 bps a side and there is no spread to pay. The same trade then needs 37% on 5-minute bars. The work is getting filled.
  • Be careful with events. A 5-minute bar inside a liquidation cascade moves several times its median. That is an occasion, not a cadence.
Costs change. Recompute them from time to time.

Spreads widen and narrow with the market. SOL's was 0.2 bps from 6 August to 4 September 2026 and 1.0 over the last 30 days.

The numbers on this page are medians over July 2025 to November 2026. Yours, today, can be different.

What this does not say

Nothing about where to enter, and nothing about an edge. Break-even is the floor, not a result. Whether a rule clears it is answered out of sample, and only there.
